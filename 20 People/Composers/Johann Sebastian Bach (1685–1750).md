- Part of:
	- [[Baroque period (≈1600–1750)]]
- Biography:
	- German composer and musician of the late Baroque period.
	- Worked as an organist, court musician, and church music director.
	- His music is closely connected to Lutheran church music, keyboard practice, dance forms, and contrapuntal craft.
- Core traits:
	- Counterpoint:
		- Bach's music often works as several independent melodic lines sounding together.
		- Each line can feel meaningful on its own, but together they create harmony and form.
	- Harmony:
		- His harmony has a strong sense of direction, tension, and resolution.
		- Even short passages often feel like they are moving toward a clear tonal goal.
	- Form:
		- Bach combines strict structure with musical flow.
		- Dance forms, fugues, preludes, chorales, and suites are shaped with strong internal logic.
	- Sacred and secular balance:
		- Much of his music is connected to Lutheran church practice.
		- At the same time, he wrote instrumental dance music, keyboard works, concertos, and chamber music.
	- Craft:
		- Bach's writing is highly economical: small motives can generate entire sections.
		- Technical discipline and expressive depth are usually inseparable in his music.
	- Listening focus:
		- Listen for independent voices rather than only melody with accompaniment.
		- Notice how dissonance creates tension and how resolution gives the music direction.
		- Notice how rhythm and form make even complex music feel organized.
